## 6.3.8 Quality assurance process

## 6.3.8.1 Purpose

The purpose of the quality assurance (QA)process is to help ensure the effective application of the organization's quality management (QM) process to the project.

QA focuses on providing confidence that quality requirements are fulfilled. Proactive analysis of the project life cycle processes and outputs is performed to help ensure that the product being produced or the service being developed is of the desired quality and that organization and project policies and procedures are followed.

NOTE Annex E presents the use of assurance claims in system assurance. Establishing an assurance case can guide QA activities and help ensure critical quality characteristics are considered.

## 6.3.8.2 Outcomes

As a result of the successful performance of the quality assurance process:

a) QA procedures, including criteria and methods for QA evaluations, are implemented.

b) Evaluations of products, services, and processes are performed, consistent with quality management policies, procedures, and requirements.

c) Results of evaluations are provided to relevant stakeholders.

d) Incidents are resolved.

e) Prioritized problems are treated.

NOTE Outcomes a) through d) align with the outcomes of the quality management process activities and tasks.

## 6.3.8.3 Activities and tasks

The project shall implement the following activities and tasks in accordance with applicable organization policies and procedures with respect to the QA process.

NOTE 1 IEEE Std 730 provides additional detail. Numerous ISO/IEC standards and guidelines on systems and software quality requirements and evaluation (SQuaRE) also define software quality characteristics.

a) Prepare for quality assurance. This activity consists of the following tasks:

1) Define a QA strategy. The strategy is consistent with the organizational QM policies and objectives and includes the following:

i) priorities for applying QA resources to processes and tasks that have the most significant impact on the quality of the delivered products and services;

ii) defined roles, responsibilities, accountabilities and authorities;

iii) evaluation criteria and methods for processes, products, and services, including criteria for product or service acceptance;

iv) activities appropriate to each supplier (including subcontractors);
